What breaks the chart

01 Make the window follow the instrument

When you switch instrument, the time window stays where it was. On the minute you don't notice. On 4h and on the daily the old window is a few hours wide: on the new scale that holds one or two candles, and the chart comes up blank.

On the daily the window lands on Sep 07 – Sep 08, on Sep 08 – 00:00, on Sep 08 – 18:17: one day, a day and a half. It happens switching to NQ, RTY, SPY, BTC, GOLD. Staying on the same instrument, 1m → 4h → D works fine: it's the instrument change that leaves the window behind.

Double-clicking doesn't fix it: it resets the vertical scale and leaves the window where it was, so the candles stay missing. The code that restores the scale is already there, it just needs to restore the window too.

On instrument change, recompute the window on that instrument's most recent data. And make the double-click recompute the window as well, not just the scale.

02 Open on the latest data

On open the chart doesn't show the present: at 18:00 New York time ES opens on 04:30–08:30, thirteen hours behind. Whoever walks in sees an old price with nothing telling them it's old.

The cause is in how it's laid out: the window is decided on 2,497 filler bars, then the 776 real bars arrive and nobody revisits the window.

Lay out once the real bars have arrived, not before, and open on the latest data available.

03 Leave the Tide off on open

The Liquidity Tide switch turns itself back on at every page load, with FLOW and LEVELS reset to Off. Turn it off and it's on again the next time you reload, every time.

If the user turned it off, it stays off.

04 Make the walls selector match what you draw

On open the walls selector says Off and the chart carries nine wall lines with their labels, while the right-hand panel lists sixteen levels. Three loads out of three, without touching anything.

Touch the selector and things line up, but slowly: tens of seconds pass between the click and the chart updating, and in the meantime the right-hand panel has already switched lists. For that stretch the user is looking at three different versions of the same thing.

On open, apply to the chart the state the selector shows. And update chart and panel at the same moment, not half a minute apart.

05 Drop the false levels from the alert

The alert price dropdown, on ES, always ends with two entries that aren't prices of the instrument. They change from day to day — 81 and 38.64, then 51 and 41.37 — but they're always there, at the bottom of a list that isn't even sorted: 7,719.25 · 7,706 · 7,736 · 7,751 · 7,681 · 7,761 · 51 · 41.37.

Pick one and the alert is created without a word: it stays armed seven thousand points away from price and will never fire.

Drop the foreign entries, sort the list, and refuse a level that sits seven thousand points from price.

06 Clean the chart up when you leave the multi-chart

Leaving the Command Deck, the single chart comes back with five settings changed on their own.

Clockstays on 09:20 ET while the LIVE button is lit
Expirygoes from All to 0DTE
Wallsgo from Off to Top 15
Instrumentgoes from SPY to ES, the first window's
Metricthe selector says GEX, the legend at the bottom says CHARM

The last one is the dangerous one: you're reading a CHARM map believing it's GEX, and the only place that says so is a small line at the bottom of the page. It also happens on leaving replay.

On exit, put the single chart back the way it was before entering the multi-chart. And in any case never leave the selector and the legend naming two different metrics.

07 Fix the multi-chart

In the Command Deck the first window draws no candles except in one corner, and its CDI bar wraps onto three lines printed over the chart. The header is transparent: the price scale numbers read straight through the text. Two windows out of four show the same instrument. Response time climbs to 1,887 ms.

The status line at the bottom has words run together — cachedCEX16:00 ETju — and is cut off by the edge.

On a normal screen four charts side by side don't fit: each one is a quarter wide and none of the four is readable.

Drop the four-charts-in-a-row layout. On the others: give every window the room for its own command bar instead of printing it over the chart, put a background behind the headers, and rewrite the status line with spaces and a number of minutes in place of <1m.

08 The CVD is an empty strip

The CVD takes a hundred and forty pixels of screen and writes one line inside them: CVD +4 contracts · zero is off the scale. Nothing drawn, bar a dash at the far right. The software announces it can't draw instead of rescaling.

And on switching it on the chart's time axis ends up under the LIVE/REPLAY bar: it recovers on its own, but for a few seconds the chart has no hours on it.

If the value is off the scale, rescale instead of saying so. And reserve the panel's room before opening it, so the axis doesn't end up under the bar.

09 Move the replay clock

Replay starts now and the candles advance. The clock doesn't: in the bar and in the header it keeps saying 09:00 ET while the candle counter is already at 09:10. Whoever watches the clock thinks it's still stuck and presses PLAY again.

Advance the clock along with the candles, in the bar and in the header.

10 A pass on the edges

Four things you see at first glance.

PRICES THAT MATTER gets cut: the last row runs off the bottom of the screen halfway through, with the response-time text landing on top of it. The panel scrolls, but nothing says it scrolls.

Axis labels get truncated when the window starts mid-label: :30 instead of 23:30, 16: instead of 16:00, 17:(, 18:0.

The wall tooltip runs off the chart, covers the price scale and cuts its last line of text in half.

The bar counter is one instrument behind: on GOLD it reads 776, which is SPX's number; on ES it reads 1,377, which is GOLD's.

Leave a margin at the bottom of the prices panel. Don't print a label that doesn't fit whole. Keep the tooltip inside the chart. And update the bar counter when the instrument changes.

11 The date on the axis is in another timezone

At 20:00 New York time the axis doesn't write 20:00: it writes Sep 10, tomorrow's date. Eight in the evening in New York is midnight UTC, and the day change is computed in UTC while the hours are in ET. Whoever reads the axis sees tomorrow's date in the middle of the evening's hours.

Compute the day change in the same timezone the hours are written in.
