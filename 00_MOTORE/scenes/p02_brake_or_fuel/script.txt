Two supports. <break time="0.2s"/> One held. <break time="0.2s"/> One collapsed. <break time="0.5s"/> On a normal chart, they look exactly the same. <break time="0.3s"/> But these are dealer walls: levels where options dealers are estimated to carry large gamma exposure. <break time="0.4s"/> A red wall is long gamma. As price falls into it, dealers tend to buy, which can slow the drop. <break time="0.4s"/> A magenta wall is short gamma. There, dealers may have to sell as price falls, which can speed the drop up. <break time="0.4s"/> Neither is a guarantee. A red wall can break, and price can bounce off a magenta one. <break time="0.4s"/> Volwick colors every wall by estimated dealer gamma, so you see what kind of level sits near price, like a real institutional trader.
