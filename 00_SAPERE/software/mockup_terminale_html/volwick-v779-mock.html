How to read Sightline

Six minutes. Read it once and the screen stops being a wall of colour.

Sightline shows you one thing: where the dealers who sold the options are stuck, and what that does to price.

When someone buys an option, a dealer sells it. That dealer now carries a risk they didn't want, so they hedge it by buying or selling the underlying. Those hedges are mechanical, they are large, and they happen at the same prices every day. That is what you are looking at.

The three things on the screen

The wallsHorizontal lines where dealer hedging piles up. Price tends to slow down or turn there.
The mapThe coloured background. Green means hedging pushes price back to the middle, red means it pushes it away.
The TideWho is winning right now, buyers or sellers, read from the order flow rather than from options.
The CDIThe strip under the chart. Real money going into calls and puts, minute by minute.

How a session usually goes

You open Sightline, you look at the right-hand panel to get the bias and the two levels that matter, and then you wait for price to reach one of the walls. The wall is the event. Everything else is context.

A wall is a price where dealers hold a lot of exposure. The more they hold, the harder they hedge, and the more price reacts.

Reading a wall label

GEX · ★★ 7705.6 · −23M · (7700)

7705.6 is where the line sits on this instrument. (7700) is the SPX strike it comes from, because ES tracks SPX with a carry offset. −23M is how much dealer P&L moves for every one-point move in SPX. ★★ means it is one of the biggest on the screen.

Stars

No star is an ordinary level. One star is worth watching. Two stars is a level that has moved price before and will probably move it again. Count stars, not decimals.

The sign

Positive, long gammaDealers sell into strength and buy into weakness. Price gets pinned. Expect the level to hold.
Negative, short gammaDealers sell into weakness and buy into strength. Breaks run. Expect the level to break hard or not at all.

How many to show

Top 5 is enough for most days. Top 15 turns the chart into a ladder and stops meaning anything. If you cannot name why a line is on your screen, take it off.

The coloured background is the same information as the walls, spread across price and time instead of drawn as lines.

The colours

Green hedging pulls price back in. Ranges, fades, mean reversion.

Red hedging pushes price away. Trends, breakouts, air pockets.

Where the colour changes sign is the line that matters. That is the edge of the pinned zone, and it is usually where the day's character changes.

The four maps

GammaThe default. How hard dealers hedge at each price, right now.
CharmHow that hedging drifts as the day runs out. Strongest in the last two hours.
VegaWhat happens if implied volatility moves. Useful around events.
VolgaWhere a volatility move would accelerate. Rarely needed intraday.

One map at a time, on purpose. Two of them on top of each other tells you nothing.

The Liquidity Tide replaces the candles with a ribbon that shows who is actually getting filled.

Green ribbon, buyers are lifting offers. Red ribbon, sellers are hitting bids. Thickness is size. When the ribbon goes pale and thin, nobody is doing anything and the next move is noise.

Where it works

Futures and SPY. On index symbols there is no tick-by-tick tape to read, so the Tide has nothing to draw. It stays off there, and the chart tells you why.

The white marks

A white dash on the price means one print was far bigger than the ones around it. Somebody with size stepped in at that price. They are worth remembering for the rest of the session.

Use it for timing, not direction The Tide tells you whether a level is being defended right now. It does not tell you where price is going next. Pair it with a wall.

The strip under the chart is the Cumulative Delta Impact: money going into calls and puts, minute by minute, converted into the pressure it puts on price.

Green bars above the line, call buying. Red bars below, put buying. The height is dollars of premium, not contracts, so one big trade looks big.

Reading it

A run of green under a falling price means somebody is buying the dip with options while the tape looks weak. That disagreement is the signal. When the CDI agrees with price, it is just confirmation.

Which book

SPXW is the index book, SPY 0DTE is the ETF book. They usually agree. When they do not, the index book is the one that moves futures.

The label says when the data was last refreshed. Outside market hours it will say the last update of the session, which is normal.

The panel on the right is the whole screen written out in words. If you only have thirty seconds, read that and nothing else.

Direction

A bias and a conviction number. Low conviction means the ingredients disagree, and you should treat the levels as a range rather than a trade.

The plan

The two levels that bracket the day, what to do when price reaches them, and where the idea is wrong. It is written as an instruction on purpose: if you cannot follow it as written, it is not a plan you should take.

Prices that matter

The walls again, as a list, sorted by price with the current one highlighted. The number on the right is dealer exposure at that level. Use it to decide which wall to care about when two are close together.

Replay plays a past session back at speed, with the walls and the map as they were at that moment, not as they are now.

Pick a time, press play, and the chart moves forward with the clock. It is the only honest way to check whether a level would have worked, because the levels themselves move during the day.

Alerts

An alert fires when price comes within a set distance of a level. Pick the level from the list, pick the distance, and leave it armed. The list only offers levels that exist on the instrument you are watching.

Replay is not live While replay is running everything on the screen is historical, including the right-hand panel. The bar says so. Come back to LIVE before you act on anything.

Four ways to misread this screen.

Trading a wall in the middle of nowhere

A wall matters when price arrives at it. A wall forty points away is information about the shape of the day, not an entry.

Turning everything on

Map, Tide, CVD, walls at Top 15 and bot marks all at once is not more information, it is less. Two layers is usually the right number.

Reading the map as a forecast

Green does not mean price goes up. It means moves get absorbed. Red does not mean price falls. It means moves get amplified. The map describes behaviour, not direction.

Ignoring the expiry

0DTE walls are enormous in the morning and gone by the afternoon. All-expiry walls move slowly and hold for days. Know which one you are looking at before you size a trade around it.
